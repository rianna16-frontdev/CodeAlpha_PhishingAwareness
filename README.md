# CodeAlpha_PhishingAwareness

**Task 2: Phishing Awareness Training** | CodeAlpha Cyber Security Internship

A presentation that teaches people how to recognise, avoid and report phishing attacks, including fake emails, fake websites and the social engineering tactics behind them.

## Contents
- `Phishing_Awareness_Training.pptx` (also available as PDF): the full presentation

## What the training covers
1. **What is phishing?** Definition, why it works, and common types (email phishing, spear phishing, smishing, vishing, whaling, clone phishing).
2. **How to recognise phishing emails**: suspicious sender addresses, urgent or threatening language, generic greetings, unexpected attachments, mismatched links, spelling and formatting errors.
3. **How to spot fake websites**: look-alike domains, missing or misleading HTTPS, odd login pages, and how to check a URL before clicking.
4. **Social engineering tactics**: urgency, fear, authority, curiosity, greed, impersonation and pretexting.
5. **Real-world examples**: well-known phishing campaigns and what made them convincing.
6. **Best practices to stay safe**:
   - Verify the sender and hover over links before clicking
   - Never share passwords or OTPs
   - Use multi-factor authentication and a password manager
   - Keep software updated
   - Report suspicious messages instead of ignoring them
7. **What to do if you were phished**: change passwords, enable MFA, notify IT or the bank, and monitor your accounts.
8. **Interactive quiz**: questions that test whether you can tell phishing from legitimate messages.

## Who is this for?
Students, employees and anyone who uses email, messaging apps or the web.

## Learning outcomes
After this training, the audience can:
- Identify the warning signs of a phishing message or website
- Understand the psychological tricks attackers use
- Respond correctly when they receive or fall for a phishing attempt

## Disclaimer
This material is for education and awareness only. The examples are described to help people defend themselves and are not instructions for carrying out attacks.
